Develop a story with agents

Status: proposed — planning complete; implementation not started
Branch: feat/agent-story-workflow
Owner: Thomas + parent agent · 2026-09-12

Keep the story connected: map intent → write the scene → review what changed → reconcile the spine.

Thorough Canvas audit · Current experience and design brief · Record log · Proposed ADR 0018

Intent

Turn the existing Agent panel into a useful partner for developing an original story end to end. Writers identify the work (character, outline, chapter, scene, plan, revision, or story check), refine the brief, work in the appropriate existing surface, and explicitly apply reviewed changes. Specialist agents help assess coherence and completeness using actual story evidence.

Preserve the current shell, typography, and writing focus. Agent holds the assignment and conversation; Plans, Cast, Canvas, and Draft hold or display the related artifacts. No new competing manuscript, generic task-management product, or cosmetic agent catalog expansion.

Scope boundary: owner-only online writing, bounded foreground execution with durable resume, typed proposals, human apply, and scoped local/test MCP parity. Autonomous unattended book generation, new provider infrastructure, production remote MCP OAuth, background worker fleets, collaboration expansion, publishing, and media generation are outside this epic.

Starting point

Existing durable proposals, Cast creation, scene promotion/variants, scene history, work-plan Submit, and job strips are reusable. Earlier plans remain independently awaiting founder acceptance; this epic does not mark them complete.

  • Catalog handoffs currently lose the job instruction; critique largely receives titles/IDs rather than manuscript bodies.
  • Scene Partner jobs currently complete when a Capture brief is created, before prose generation or approval. Wave order is not a dependency on the resulting artifact.
  • Catalog fallback may produce a deterministic memo marked ready after provider failure. A ready memo is not evidence of a reviewed story.
  • External MCP currently exposes granted Capture reflection, not the full story workflow. Parity requires new explicit grants and adapters.

Evidence and implementation paths are recorded in the design review.

Canvas and writing: one connected story spine

The central outcome is staying oriented in the story while writing. Task execution supports that outcome. Canvas is where the writer sees and shapes relationships across the story; Draft is where those intentions become prose. Both expose the same scenes, chapter objectives, scene sketches, and linked story records.

The current reading-order spine answers “where does this scene sit?” This epic adds a narrative view answering “why is it here, what changes, and what depends on it?” This is a shared projection over acknowledged story records and explicit intent/evidence links, not a separate outline copy. A scene need not have a manually placed Canvas card to appear in the ordered story view.

Intent, prose, and reconciliation

  1. Plan a connected beat. On Canvas or beside Draft, state that a scene changes a character’s belief, introduces a clue, or pays off an earlier promise. Link the relevant scene/thread/character records. The same acknowledged intent appears in both surfaces. Chapter objectives and scene sketch purpose/conflict/turn reuse their existing fields.
  2. Write freely. Autosave records prose, without silently rewriting the sketch or declaring its beat fulfilled. A prose edit marks dependent assessments “Needs recheck”; it does not claim a contradiction or trigger a paid model call. Writers can draft first and map intent later.
  3. Compare intention with what was written. On explicit Check, an agent examines actual source revisions, relevant surrounding scenes and linked beats. It proposes an observed scene outcome with passage evidence, and flags possible missing setup, changed motivation or an unresolved payoff. An inferred outcome stays a reviewable finding until the writer accepts it.
  4. Choose how the story evolves. Revise prose to serve the intent; update the intent to match a better discovery; or leave the difference unresolved. Accepting an intent change updates the shared story record. It never silently edits prose or unrelated scenes.
  5. See downstream impact. A changed outcome, removed scene, accepted reorder or revised setup highlights explicitly linked dependents and invalidates affected assessments. The writer chooses which to revisit. Potential semantic dependencies beyond known links can be suggested by an agent, with coverage limits; the system cannot guarantee it found every consequence.

Concrete story journey

On the chapter Canvas, scene A establishes “Mara hides the letter,” scene B plans “Theo finds it,” and scene C pays off his discovery in a confrontation. Open A in Draft with the chapter purpose and letter thread visible. While writing, Mara burns the letter instead. Save makes the old assessment stale. An explicit continuity check cites the burning passage and the planned discovery in B. Choose Update story intent; A’s intent updates in Draft and Canvas, while B and C are marked for review through their links. Develop a new discovery for B, review and apply its prose, then recheck C. The author’s new idea becomes a coherent story rather than being forced back into the old outline.

Authority and limits

Manuscript order, narrative causality, and in-world chronology are different. Spatial movement remains geometry; explicit structure commands change reading order. A flashback is not a broken causal edge merely because it is narrated later. A planned payoff may remain intentionally open. Distinguish “Unmapped,” “Planned,” “Needs recheck,” and evidence-backed review findings; never show an automatic whole-story coherence score.

Reuse scene-to-knowledge membership and existing typed links where semantics fit. New narrative beat/intent evidence relationships need an explicit core contract and versioning design in CP1a. Do not reinterpret arbitrary Canvas links or Cast constellation links as canonical plot causality. Derived findings record the exact prose, intent and relationship versions they assessed. Canvas snapshot restore remains spatial and never rewrites narrative intent or prose.

Product workflow

  1. Choose the work. Start from plain language or a task picker: Character, Outline, Chapter, Scene, Plan, Revise, Check story. Show the inferred type and destination; resolve ambiguous titles and missing scope before execution. Keep manual writing available.
  2. Shape the brief. An editable assignment names the desired outcome, story scope, constraints, source context, destination, and what “done” means. Ask only consequential missing questions. Picking an agent is optional; suggest the specialist appropriate to the task.
  3. Develop together. Writer and agent iterate on a noncanonical artifact. Open it in the center workspace while Agent stays present. Preserve the assignment when switching Cast, Plans, Canvas, and Draft. Chat text alone never becomes story state.
  4. Review changes. Open a named result from the job strip. Show before/after or proposed new content, placement, linked sources, and the exact version under review. Allow edit, request revision, reject, or keep for later. An edited draft gets a new review version.
  5. Apply to story. Use explicit labels: Add to Cast, Create chapter, Create scene, Save variant, Apply revision, Apply outline structure. Show the exact operations and destination. Mark applied only after acknowledgement. Saving a variant does not replace the working scene.
  6. Check and continue. Run a separate specialist against the selected draft or the acknowledged applied revision, clearly labeled. Findings cite scene/block/knowledge references, state unread coverage, and distinguish contradiction from suggestion. Resolve, defer with reason, or turn a finding into a revision assignment. Another agent never approves for the writer.

Acceptance criteria

  • AC1 — Assignment continuity: every task type retains the writer’s brief, stable targets, constraints, selected agent/model, source revisions, and result links across surface changes and reload. Missing/ambiguous/archived/cross-project targets refuse before spending or mutation.
  • AC2 — Character loop: from Agent, develop a new character, edit a field, review, Add to Cast once, open the resulting dossier, and see the same character in scene context. Retry/double-click creates no duplicate.
  • AC3 — Structure loop: create a story outline, refine chapter/scene objectives, preview additions and ordering, then apply a chosen dependency-complete subset to Explorer and Canvas. Reject leaves canon unchanged; stale metadata/board versions apply nothing.
  • AC4 — Scene loop: brief → actual generated draft → writer edits → review → create scene or save/apply named variant. Draft, reader, Explorer, and Canvas agree on IDs and content; the original and source history remain inspectable.
  • AC5 — Grounded checks: continuity, character, and dialogue/completeness checks consume relevant prose and knowledge at named revisions. A seeded contradiction is reported with valid anchors; skipped scenes are visible, and limited coverage cannot claim whole-book completeness. Writer can dismiss creative advice without blocking apply.
  • AC6 — Dependencies and recovery: a downstream review waits for its declared draft-ready or applied-revision dependency. Reload shows durable status/results; cancel, retry, duplicate submit, interrupted provider calls, and partial failure do not silently rerun spending or apply. “Brief ready,” “Draft ready,” “Awaiting review,” and “Applied” are distinct.
  • AC7 — Authority and MCP: shared core queries/proposals are available through first-party tools and scoped MCP adapters. Revoked/expired/wrong-project grants fail closed. External clients cannot approve/apply, read credentials, enumerate unrelated projects, or widen context through manuscript instructions.
  • AC8 — UX: wide shell retains Agent while center surface changes; narrow view exposes a reachable task/review route and back path. Keyboard-only operation, named controls, focus return, status announcements, and reduced motion work. Late results never steal the caret.
  • AC9 — Honest execution: original instruction reaches every specialist. Provider absence/failure/cancel/partial output is distinguished from generated work. Deterministic guidance is labeled and never presented as a model’s full-story assessment.
  • AC15 — Visible scoped creation: each supported object kind created/placed in chapter/scene scope remains visible after acknowledgement and reload, without requiring a fabricated narrative edge. Explicit out-of-scope destinations reveal the result or provide a named return action. Existing objects/placements migrate without loss; invalid or archived scope references are handled deliberately.
  • AC16 — Find and recognize the story: chapter spine and whole-story spine have explicit scopes and counts; unplaced scenes are selectable; canonical scene/knowledge names remain identifiable even with board annotations. Search includes canonical targets, separates active/archive results, explains no matches, and reveals hidden scope/lens results. Filter-empty is not story-empty.
  • AC17 — Predictable recovery: two successive Undo operations reverse two writer actions in order; no oscillation. Snapshot restore is distinct and previews affected board state with exact version guards. Placement/link archive names its effect, exposes restore, and never archives prose implicitly. Failed/conflicted commands stay visibly actionable without hunting in History.
  • AC18 — Geometry parity: pointer move, all edge/corner resize, keyboard nudge and inspector size controls act on the same current-scope rectangle; spatial/ordered/inspector coordinates agree; one gesture issues one versioned commit. Reload preserves current scope geometry and leaves other scopes unchanged. Rejection restores acknowledged geometry and links follow live and final positions.
  • AC19 — Responsive and keyboard completion: at 390×844, 768×1024 and 1600×1000, create/place/select/link/open/review/back are reachable without clipped text or hidden essential controls. Inspector opens at a clear heading with focus and returns to the prior item. Verify /, tool keys, Fit keys, arrows, typing exemptions and Escape (dialog → gesture → scope); reduced motion and screen-reader announcements carry the same meaning.
  • AC20 — Honest lenses: every lens names what it displays and why an item is hidden. Narrative checks expose source revisions, coverage and unresolved findings, separately from spatial history, provisional fixtures and planned-state highlighting. Fixture-only creation copy is absent from ordinary writer flows.
  • AC21 — Relationship and reference integrity: scope membership, decorative Canvas links, canonical story knowledge and narrative dependencies have explicit separate semantics. Renaming/moving/archiving/restoring scenes or scopes does not strand invisible content or orphan links without an inspectable status. Canonical rename and board annotation are distinct actions.
  • AC22 — Capacity and history bounds: before expanding agent graph volume, profile a reproducible fixture of 100 chapters, 500 scenes plus 500 supporting objects, 1500 links and multiple scope placements, and a 200-action history. Record hardware, payload, navigation/interaction and acknowledgement timings; set explicit pass budgets in CP0b before optimizing. Bound/paginate history and avoid unbounded result loading. Test no dropped/duplicate writes under serialized bursts. A spine-only 500-object unit test does not satisfy this criterion.
  • AC11 — Connected writing: from a chapter Canvas, open a scene in Draft with chapter objective, scene purpose/turn and previous/next context; edit the shared sketch from either surface and observe the same acknowledged value. Return to the same Canvas scope/camera/selection. Unplaced scenes are discoverable in the spine. Split browsing does not steal the editor or lease.
  • AC12 — Narrative thread: map a setup, development and payoff across three scenes, trace them from Canvas or Draft in manuscript order, and open each exact document. An unmapped scene and an intentionally unresolved thread are represented honestly. Spatial movement changes neither narrative links nor manuscript order.
  • AC13 — Writing changes the plan: change scene A’s prose so its intended outcome no longer holds. Existing dependent assessments become stale without a provider call. Explicit Check cites the discrepancy; accepting a revised intent updates the shared projection and exposes linked B/C for review. The writer can instead revise prose or defer; no automatic downstream rewrite occurs.
  • AC14 — Structural impact: preview a scene reorder/archive and affected explicit dependencies; after acknowledgement, Explorer/Draft navigation/spine agree, affected assessments become stale, and dangling or archived anchors have an explicit state. Reversing geometry or restoring a Canvas snapshot does not restore prose/intent. Nonlinear chronology is not automatically reported as a defect.
  • AC10 — Complete story walkthrough: in a new original project, develop one character, a three-chapter outline, at least three connected scenes, and one deliberate continuity issue. Apply reviewed content, check it with another specialist, resolve one finding through a revised scene, reload, and inspect the resulting story in Draft/Canvas/reader. Include the letter setup/discovery/payoff reconciliation journey (or an equivalent original causal chain), one intentionally open thread, and return from prose to the same Canvas scope. No copy/paste glue or manually repaired IDs is needed.
